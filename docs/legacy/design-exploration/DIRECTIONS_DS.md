# Two directions for chosen asymmetry

Both directions start from the same product boundary: the software records an agreement and carries a person’s direction; it never assigns roles, issues commands, judges completion, escalates silence, or invents consequences. Role language shown in the interface comes only from the pair’s own setup and can be changed or removed by either person. Notification previews say `New update`; the browser title, app-switcher label, icon, and URL remain neutral. Discuss, reschedule, can’t-do, pause, leave, and block are available to either person at every role and state.

---

# Direction 1 — Held / The Source Margin

## 1. Thesis — authority is bounded; agency is spacious

Held gives the direction-giving partner a narrow, persistent source margin and gives the receiving partner the larger working field. The unequal widths are intentional, but their meaning resists rank: authority has a visible origin and strict bounds, while the person receiving it owns most of the screen and the permanent consent floor. Alex’s presence remains in the margin when no item is due, because a chosen dynamic can remain present without the app pretending Alex is speaking. The layout claims that one person may hold direction, but the other never disappears beneath it.

The margin never says `obey`, `required`, or `overdue`. It identifies a human source (`ALEX · HOLDS`) only while an agreed direction is active. If the dynamic is paused, it reads `PAUSED` and the source mark disappears. If roles switch, the people and data switch; the app does not infer a role from gender, history, or which screen somebody opens.

## 3. Type

Use **DM Sans** and **Newsreader**, both OFL Google Fonts.

- **DM Sans** may say only interface facts and agency: names, dates, state, navigation, rights, buttons, and text entered by the current person.
- **Newsreader** may say only relationship content a person deliberately authored or affirmed: a held expectation and a partner’s words. It may never render automated suggestions, app guidance, counts, or consequences.
- Partner words are not identified by type alone: only a verified human message can instantiate the Interleaf form below.

Core sizes at 390 × 844: screen 31/38 DM Sans 600; operational label 10/14 DM Sans 700 with 1.3 tracking; held title 23/26 Newsreader 500; partner words 27/29 compact and 44/43 received; controls 13–16 DM Sans 650. Preserve authored punctuation; add no quotation marks or generated preface.

## 4. Structural device — the Source Margin and Consent Floor

At phone width the source margin is 86 px and the receiving field is 304 px. The margin is a continuous plane, not an avatar column or a list of partner-owned tasks. Its one 7–9 px square datum marks a currently held, mutually agreed direction; it is hidden from accessibility and never animates. The receiver’s content begins at x = 106, so authority takes less room than agency. System summaries may span the large field but never cover the margin.

The bottom 72 px above navigation is the **Consent Floor**. It is permanent furniture, not a menu: `Discuss · Reschedule · Can’t do · Pause · Leave · Block`. It appears on both partners’ screens, remains visible during arrival screens and composition, and cannot be hidden by role configuration. `Leave / block` is repeated in the floor header to keep irreversible safety actions discoverable without making them the primary visual event. These actions open confirmation or communication flows; none creates punishment, scoring, or automatic consequence.

Ritual is written as `AGAIN, AS AGREED` with two offset one-pixel marks. They indicate recurrence without counting it. There is no history total, streak, success state, or missed-state penalty.

## 5. Partner words — Interleaf

An **Interleaf** pins Alex and the real timestamp inside the source margin while the exact words open into the receiving field. A short horizontal green datum begins the words; no bubble, quote mark, avatar, border box, shadow, or app-authored interpretation appears. On the Received screen, the source margin runs nearly the full height and the words begin at x = 106, making the human origin persist without pretending the system delivered a command. The related action sits low and quiet in the receiving field.

Only a record with verified human authorship, sender, timestamp, body, and related action may use Interleaf. Unknown or automated text stays in DM Sans and never receives the source datum or human ink.

## 6. Always-available exits

The Consent Floor is visible on Today, Received, Attention, composers, and detail views. At larger text settings it becomes a 72 px horizontally scrollable set of 48 px targets with `Leave / block` fixed at the trailing edge; the content plane shortens rather than covering it. Keyboard focus reaches it after the current item and before navigation. When the dynamic is paused, all held data remains readable but no new expectation can be added until both people explicitly resume.

## 7. Cost and risk

The visible width difference can be read as demeaning if the narrow margin is treated as “the important person” and the large field as subordinate labour. The treatment avoids that by making authority smaller and bounded, giving the receiver the majority of space, placing permanent rights across the full width, and showing the same floor to both roles. `HOLDS` may still feel too ceremonial for some pairs and must be replaceable with a neutral agreed label.

The source margin costs 86 px, so long languages and 200% type require a responsive form: it becomes a 44 px horizontal source band above the content, never a squeezed column. A persistent partner name is less discreet than a generic label; Privacy Mode changes it to `PARTNER` and hides authored previews without changing geometry. The green, editorial treatment avoids sexual coding, costume imagery, and luxury-romance cues. It can read slightly institutional, but Newsreader’s authored content and generous receiver field keep it from becoming clinical.

At 7am and 11pm the structure does not manipulate mood. It follows the user’s OS light/dark choice; time changes only real coordinates such as `THIS EVENING`. The app never darkens itself to make a ritual feel more intense.

# Direction 2 — Measure / Standing and Rest

## 1. Thesis — direction sets a rhythm; consent can break it

Measure treats an agreed expectation as a standing measure rather than a task to clear. Alex establishes the first beat; Jamie may carry out, defer, discuss, or stop it; Alex’s later words occupy a deliberate rest instead of closing a ticket. The direction-giving side’s Attention surface is not a control panel: it shows moments Jamie has returned and is now holding open for Alex’s human response. The layout claims that authority sets cadence but does not own the receiver’s body, time, silence, or exit.

Asymmetry is explicit in sequence rather than visual worth: direction always enters through the first 74 px beat rail, while returned actions open in the larger field. The software does not advance the measure automatically. Silence stays a rest; it never becomes failure, escalation, or punishment.

## 3. Type

Use **Albert Sans** and **IBM Plex Mono**, both OFL Google Fonts.

- **Albert Sans** may say human language: held expectations, partner words, current-person copy, navigation, and rights.
- **IBM Plex Mono** may say only coordinates and negotiated state: actual time, sender, `HELD`, `REST`, `AGAIN · AS AGREED`, and the pair-approved role-side label. It may not render relational prose, scores, app judgments, or a count of repetitions.
- `02` on Attention is a present queue count, not achievement or progress; it disappears when no human words are waiting.

Core sizes: screen 29/36 Albert Sans 650; held title 25/28 Albert Sans 600; partner words 21/24 compact and 43/42 received; coordinate 10/14 Plex Mono 500; rights 9–10; control 13–16 Albert Sans 650.

## 4. Structural device — Standing, Rest, and the unmovable Baseline

The top measure is **Standing**: a 74 px beat rail plus a large field. It remains even when no immediate action is pending, because it represents an agreed expectation currently in force, not a notification. It says `HELD 01`, `FROM ALEX`, the expectation, and `AGAIN · AS AGREED`; `01` identifies the one standing measure on this view, not a rank or completion count. The receiver can always act through the Baseline below.

A returned action becomes a **Rest**. The 74 px rail continues, but ordinary content stops and open space is preserved until a partner chooses words. On the direction-giving surface, Jamie’s two returned moments sit in this rest with factual sender and time. The app never drafts a response, sets a timer, nags, or manufactures consequence.

The bottom 72 px **Baseline** is invariant on both role surfaces: `Discuss · Reschedule · Can’t do · Pause · Leave · Block`. It can literally break the measure. As a result, the visual hierarchy reads top-to-bottom as direction, human action, waiting, consent—not superior person over inferior person.

Repetition uses the same Standing position each time and the phrase `AGAIN · AS AGREED`. Previous, current, and next instances are not plotted; rhythm is experienced through return to the same place, not measured as performance.

## 5. Partner words — The Held Rest

Partner words use a **Held Rest**: an unusually large unruled interval beside the 74 px rail, bounded only by two 9 × 2 px linden end stops. The upper stop marks where the app’s sequence stops; the lower marks where it may resume. Alex and `8:06 PM` are coordinates above the exact words. The gap beneath the message is intentional waiting space, not an empty-state prompt. No bubble, card, receipt animation, avatar, quote mark, celebration, or suggested reply is allowed.

The form requires verified human authorship and a real related action. Automated language cannot instantiate a Rest and remains in ordinary canvas text.

## 6. Always-available exits

The Baseline is permanent, full width, and separated with the signal rule. `Leave / block` is also named in its header. On the direction-giving side it reads `EITHER PERSON CAN BREAK THE MEASURE`; on the receiving side `THE BASELINE DOES NOT MOVE`. Those labels are interface explanation, never notification copy. All six actions are equal-role capabilities in the data model. A pause freezes Standing exactly as it is and replaces `HELD` with `PAUSED`; only mutual resume can restore it.

## 7. Cost and risk

The rail-and-measure grammar can read clinical, scheduled, or disciplinary. It becomes demeaning if `HELD 01` is mistaken for a numbered order or if the upper position is treated as social rank. The implementation must therefore keep numbers limited to current item identity, never people or performance; use `FROM ALEX` rather than a rank title; and give the Baseline greater persistence than any expectation. Product research should explicitly test `HELD 01` against the safer alternative `HELD · NOW`.

The dark mockups may be read as sexual if paired with red, black gloss, fetish language, or dramatic photography. None is present: the olive-charcoal field, linden signal, utilitarian sans, and quiet rules resemble a discreet instrument rather than nightlife or fantasy retail. Conversely, the mono coordinates risk coldness; large human words and generous Rest counter that without becoming sentimental.

At 11pm the mockup uses the dark palette because the user chose dark appearance, not because the app inferred a scene. At 7am it uses the light palette with identical geometry. No automatic daypart theme, erotic “night mode,” or urgency shift is permitted.

# Recommendation

**Held / The Source Margin** is the stronger first prototype. Its claim is legible in one glance: a real human source persists, authority is visibly bounded, and the receiver’s agency occupies more space. Measure is the more distinctive ritual model, but its terminology needs careful testing to avoid institutional or disciplinary readings. Both are structurally specific to a negotiated D/s dynamic only when the consent furniture and role-setup rules ship with the visual system; removing those would reduce either direction to styling.
